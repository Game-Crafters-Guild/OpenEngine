---
title: Ocean surface detail, rivers and spray
description: The ocean features that add to the analytic surface (a normal-mapped surface, water-body materials, spline rivers, accumulated shadows, crest spray, and reflected caustics), the shallow clarity window, and how the water reflects the sky.
---

The ocean renders an analytic surface by default. This guide covers the features that add to it: a detailed surface driven by a normal texture, materials on individual water bodies, continuous spline rivers, accumulated water shadows, and crest spray. Each one is off until you use it, so an ocean that uses none of them renders and costs what it did without them. The one exception is the shallow clarity window below, which is on by default. The last section describes how the water reflects the sky and the scene.

## Detailed surface

Assign a tangent-space normal map to `OceanSurface.NormalTexture` (X/Y slopes in red/green; RGB or BC5). With it, the surface filters its ripples by the pixel footprint, reflects with the rippled normal, gives foam relief and roughness, adds a submerged bubble layer, and widens the sun glint where ripples are unresolved. Without it every one of those terms is the analytic surface's.

| Field | Effect |
|---|---|
| `NormalsStrength`, `NormalsScale` | Ripple strength and frequency. At scale 1 the nearest texture tile is 8 m, blending to larger tiles with distance. |
| `FoamTexture`, `FoamScale` | Foam dissolve pattern and its tile size in meters. Also used by the analytic surface. |
| `FoamNormalStrength` | Relief taken from the foam pattern; 0 keeps foam flat. |
| `FoamRoughness` | Roughness of white foam, independent of the water. |
| `FoamBubbleCoverage`, `FoamBubbleParallax` | Visibility and apparent depth of the submerged bubbles. |

A current advects the texture detail in two overlapping phases, so the pattern never stretches however long the current runs.

## Materials on water bodies

Assign an `.oceanpreset` to `OceanWaterBody.MaterialOverride` or `OceanPolygonWaterBody.MaterialOverride`. A preset is sparse: values it omits are the ocean's. It can set the water, scatter, shallow and foam colors, fog density, refraction, reflection, index of refraction, Fresnel power, roughness, specular, ripple and foam detail, and its own `Surface.FoamTexture` / `Surface.NormalTexture` (an asset GUID; an empty string selects the procedural fallback). Where footprints overlap, the higher `MaterialPriority` wins. Waves, simulations and feature toggles stay shared. Edited preset files reload.

## Rivers from splines

Add `OceanSplineInput` next to a `SplineComponent`. The curve becomes a continuous triangle ribbon `Width` meters either side of the spline, sampled at least every `MaxSegmentLength` meters of arc. The ribbon writes into the ocean's existing fields:

| Output | Effect | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| `Flow`, `FlowSpeed`, `ReverseFlow` | A current along the curve; foam and surface detail drift downstream. | Ocean Flow on |
| `Depth`, `DepthMeters`, `DepthFeather` | A shallow band: shallow tint, shoreline foam, calmer waves and CPU depth queries. | Seabed depth available |
| `Clip`, `ClipFeather` | Cuts the surface along the curve. | Ocean ClipSurface on |
| `ConfineSurface` | Water only inside the ribbon: a river, canal or pool boundary. CPU surface queries follow it. |  |
| `UnderwaterVolume`, `UnderwaterDepth` | Extrudes a confined ribbon downward so the camera can dive into the river. | `ConfineSurface` |
| `Albedo`, `Color`, `AlbedoCoverage` | Paints the surface along the curve. | Ocean Albedo on |

The ribbon does not use the analytic source budgets, and its fields are rebuilt only when the spline, the ribbon settings, or the ocean origin change. Very sharp reversals fold a wide ribbon. Smooth the curve or narrow the ribbon there.

To run a river through terrain, give the same spline entity a `TerrainModifierVolume` with shape SplinePath and a `TerrainHeightOffsetEffect` that lowers the ground: one curve then carves the channel and carries the water. Set the spline height to the water level and make `Width` reach past the point where the banks rise above it, so the edge of a confined surface ends inside the bank instead of standing in the open as a cut edge.

## Accumulated water shadows

Assign an `.oceansettings` asset of kind Shadows (the example is `temporal_shadows.oceansettings`) to `OceanRenderer.ShadowSettings` and set `Shadows.Enabled`. The ocean then accumulates the sun's shadow in world space over time, with a hard channel for the sun glint and a soft, jittered channel for the water's scattered light. `JitterDiameter` and `HardJitterDiameter` are in meters. `TemporalWeight` and `SimulationFrequency` set how much history each update keeps. Camera cuts, paused time, and a moving sun discard the history. `OceanShadowInput` rectangles add authored shadow.

## Crest spray

`OceanSurface.Spray` tears fine droplets from raised, breaking crests once the wind exceeds `SprayWindThreshold`. Droplets are born within `SpraySpawnRadius` meters of the camera, fly with the wind under gravity, and are lit like the water: sun through the shadow map plus the sky and environment light. Raise `SprayEmissionThreshold` to keep only the strongest crests.

| Field | Effect |
|---|---|
| `SpraySpawnRate` | Crest points tested per second on each square meter of the spawn disk. |
| `SprayMaxParticles` | Droplets alive at once per view (256 to 262 144). |
| `SprayLifetime`, `SprayStartSize`, `SprayEndSize`, `SprayOpacity` | Droplet life and look. |
| `SprayUpVelocity`, `SprayWindVelocityScale` | Launch speed and how strongly the wind carries droplets. |
| `SprayGeometryIntersections`, `SprayIntersectionSpawnRate`, `SprayIntersectionBand` | Extra splashes where opaque geometry cuts the waterline. |

A calm ocean schedules no spray work once the last droplets have landed.

## Reflected caustics

`OceanSurface.ReflectedCausticsStrength` lights geometry just above the water with the caustics the surface reflects (piers, hulls, banks). `ReflectedCausticsHeight` and `ReflectedCausticsFalloff` set how far up they reach. They are off by default: a strength of 0 draws nothing, and a scene turns them on by raising it.

## Shallow clarity window

Seen from above, the surface eases its depth fog in over the first meters of the view ray's path through the water, so the seabed and its caustics stay readable in the shallows however dense the fog is. `OceanSurface.ShallowClarityDistance` is that path length in meters (default 8, range 0.01 to 50): shorter brings the deep color in sooner. `OceanSurface.ShallowClarityFloor` is the fraction of the depth fog applied where the path starts at the surface (default 0.22, range 0 to 1). The fog ramps smoothly from it up to full strength over the distance and is unscaled beyond. Unlike the features above, the window is on by default. A floor of 1 turns it off. It does not apply to the water seen from below the surface.

## Reflection

The water reflects its surroundings, weighted by the Fresnel term of the air and water boundary. The base reflectance comes from `OceanSurface.IorAir` and `IorWater` (about 0.02 at the defaults 1.0 and 1.333) and rises toward 1 at grazing angles; `FresnelPower` sets how fast it rises (5 is the physical curve). What the water reflects is the scene's environment, blurred by `Roughness`, with the planar capture blended over it when `PlanarReflections` is on and a capture was rendered this frame. Without an environment source, the procedural sky colors stand in.

| Field | Effect |
|---|---|
| `Specular` | Scales the Fresnel weight of the reflection. 1 is the full reflection; lower values let the water body show through toward the horizon. Default 1. |
| `ReflectionStrength` | Scales the whole reflection, sky and planar capture alike (default 1). |
| `PlanarReflectionStrength` | How strongly the planar capture covers the reflected environment (default 1, range 0 to 3). Above 1 it brightens the captured objects, and the sky does not show through them. |
| `ShallowRefractionReflectionSuppression` | Lowers the reflection where the seabed shows through, so caustics stay readable (default 1; 0 keeps the full reflection). |

The sun and every other light reflect off the water as off any other surface: one highlight at the water's `Roughness` (default 0.12), with the same base reflectance from `IorAir` and `IorWater`, darkened by the shadows of every light. Ripples smaller than a pixel widen it, so a rough or distant sea spreads the sun into a path of glints. `Specular` and `ReflectionStrength` scale the reflection of the surroundings only, not the highlights of lights.

Foam and painted albedo are ordinary surfaces: they take the scene's lighting and environment reflection where they cover the water, and the water's own reflection is hidden under them. The scene's other reflection sources reach the open water as they reach any surface: screen-space reflections replace the water's reflection where a trace hits, so objects reflect in the water with `PlanarReflections` off; a local reflection probe's parallax and the specular fill of an `AmbientLight` with `AffectSpecular` change the environment it reflects. The reflections of a DDGI volume do not reach the open water. The planar capture covers the environment where it captured an object, and screen-space reflections replace it there too. The web profile has neither the planar capture nor the environment on the water: there it reflects the procedural sky colors.
