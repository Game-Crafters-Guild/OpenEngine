---
title: Regional terrain grass
description: Shortening or thinning a terrain's grass inside a modifier volume, with how regions compose, the control field, placement, storage, cost and limits.
---

A terrain carries one grass profile: one blade height, one density, one set of look parameters for the whole surface. That profile has to be the wild maximum, because nothing could reduce it locally. So a village, a courtyard, or a worn path was either knee-deep in the same grass as the meadow around it, or the whole terrain had to be shortened to suit the village.

`TerrainGrassEffect` is the modifier effect that shortens or thins that profile inside a region. It rides the existing modifier volume: the same circle, rectangle, spline path, spline area, and global footprints, the same falloff, master weight, enable state, and priority as `Flatten`, `HeightOffset`, `Noise`, `Stamp`, `PaintLayer`, `SurfaceRules`, and `GroundClaim`.

## Authoring

:::steps

1. Add **Terrain: Grass Area** from the component presets, or add **Grass** to an existing modifier volume's effect stack.
2. Shape and place the volume as for any other terrain effect. The volume's falloff is what feathers the grass transition.
3. Set **Height** and **Density**. Nothing else is needed: both default to **100%**, so a region added and left alone changes nothing until you move one.

:::

| Inspector | Stored field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Height**, 0-100%, default 100% | `HeightScale`, `[0,1]`, default 1 | Blade (or card) height as a percentage of the terrain's own grass height. The field shows the absolute it lands on when one terrain carries grass. `0` admits no blades at all. |
| **Density**, 0-100%, default 100% | `DensityScale`, `[0,1]`, default 1 | Percentage of the terrain's own grass density, shown alongside the resulting blades per square meter. `0` admits no blades. Surviving blades keep the position, yaw, width, and randomness they had before the region existed, so lowering density thins a field rather than reshuffling it. |

Both controls are percentages of the terrain's grass settings, and the stored fields stay plain `[0,1]` multipliers: the percentage is presentation, the multiplier is the content.

Nonfinite values are refused by the scene schema; finite values are clamped to `[0,1]` on load, in the inspector and at gather, so a value written straight through ECS is bounded too.

:::caution[A practical floor on Height Scale]

At the shipped profile (0.72 m blades) a Height below about **50%** stops reading as short grass and starts reading as ground color: the blades are under 36 cm, which at eye height barely clears the terrain surface. Measured at a fixed pose over a fixed interior rectangle with density held at 100%, the interior's luminance contrast falls to 60% of the untouched field at 50%, 47% at 35%, and 33% at 25%. The halfway crossing sits between 35% and 50%. If a region needs to look sparser rather than shorter, lower **Density** instead. The two read differently.

:::

### How overlapping regions compose

Each terrain sample starts neutral at `(1,1)` and walks the volume stack in priority order, applying `lerp(current, target, ease(volumeWeight))` per effect. It is an ordered *target* blend, not a product: a later region with target `1` restores the full profile over an earlier suppression, which is how you cut a full-height clearing back into a thinned area. The corollary is that a neutral effect is not a no-op if it sits above a suppression. It has to stay in the stack to keep restoring.

The `ease` is a smoothstep over the volume's own falloff weight, applied to grass only. The shared weight ramps linearly from 0 at the outer edge of the falloff to 1 at the inner boundary, which is right for a height offset: the ground it produces *is* that ramp. Grass is not that ramp. Measured on a region with a 60 m inner radius and a 25 m falloff, the coverage an eye picks up is an **S-curve under either blend**, steepest in the middle of the band (r ≈ 75 m) and far shallower at both boundaries than mid-band. The linear blend still arrives at the outer edge at roughly 40% of its peak slope, which is the corner the ease softens. That shape is consistent with visible coverage saturating in a dense field: it holds near full until height × density has fallen well below 1. What the smoothstep changes is the *corners*. It has zero slope at both ends of the weight, so the slope jump where the region meets the untouched field is 1.5–2.3× smaller (0.0156 → 0.0107 per meter at the outer edge, 0.0100 → 0.0044 at the inner one, on a 50%/50% region), and the change it removes from the corners reappears in the middle, which is about 1.4× steeper. A corner is what reads as a seam, so that is the trade: gentler corners, a busier middle, and two looks that are hard to tell apart at a glance. Every other effect still ramps with the shared weight, untouched.

## Ownership and invalidation

`TerrainModifierSystem` gathers volume geometry once, then splits the grass effects out of the stack before the height/material composition hash. Editing a grass target, its enable flag, or its stack order therefore cannot move heightfield versions, splat maps, normal maps, CBT height sources, or physics recook inputs.

:::caution[Adding or removing the component is structural]

It can move a mixed volume between ECS archetypes, and equal-priority ground volumes are tie-broken by archetype gather order. On a volume that also carries height or material effects, adding a grass effect can therefore reorder those against another volume of the same priority. Give overlapping ground volumes distinct priorities when their order matters. Standalone grass volumes have no ground effects to reorder.

:::

The compositor produces an optional RG8 control field (red is height, green is density, `(255,255)` neutral), quantized after the whole ordered stack, recording the rectangles and content versions that actually changed. A neutral field stores no pixels and needs no texture. An active field that returns to neutral releases them. A fresh field tests the lowering regions' bounds before visiting any texel, so a village-sized region never scans a whole terrain.

Recomposition covers the union of a moved region's old and new bounds. A change in the surviving volume order forces a full recomposition. Removal, disabled effects and volumes, terrain recreation, and terrain re-entry after `ECS::Disabled` all reconcile against the current stack, including an empty one. The compositor reads the current authored terrain transform rather than the origin extraction publishes later in the tick. Idle frames neither recompose nor upload.

`TerrainExtractionSystem` owns the upload cache. `TerrainRenderFeature` owns the GPU textures and their deferred retirement. A new control image clears to white, but a defined white clear is not yet authored content: grass placement stays disabled for a terrain whose required field is still pending upload, image initialization, or bindless publication. A recreated unified map keeps its whole-image upload requirement across a staging failure. The atlas waits for the coarse field and for every currently assigned slot, including reassignments and generation changes.

That gate costs an author nothing in the normal case. A control texture created during extraction is published in the same tick, because the extraction clock still holds the previous tick's value while the registrar runs and the upload pass stamped that same value when it declared during the previous frame. Adding, disabling, re-enabling, and deleting a region were each measured at **zero** suppressed ticks. The gate fires only when the terrain upload pass has not declared since the previous extraction tick (a render pipeline with no such pass, or the frame one is swapped in), which is the case it exists for. `get_terrain_debug` (through the debug port) reports `grassControlSuppressedTicks`, so the question has a number rather than a screenshot.

## Placement

The placement compute samples one control field at the blade's site and multiplies the existing mask probability by density. The mask random is per-site and stable, so thinning removes a monotone subset of the same candidates instead of reshuffling the field. Height multiplies the blade height for both ribbon blades and texture cards. The global candidate budget is unchanged: a region cannot buy more grass than the terrain already pays for.

**Texture cards scale as a whole; ribbons scale in height.** A card is grass artwork on a single quad, and the renderer sizes that quad from the card's *width* — a card's height is its width, and the quad is twice as tall as it is wide. Scaling only the height was therefore dropped on the floor for cards: measured on a textured card field, a Height 50% region left the frame indistinguishable from no region at all. The region now scales the card's width, so the whole card scales, the Height control works on card grass, and the artwork keeps its proportions. A ribbon blade has no such coupling: the blade *is* the geometry, so a shorter blade is simply shorter and its width is unrelated. At 100% both are exactly the terrain's own size. What a region still cannot do is swap the artwork for genuinely different card art. That is a per-region grass profile, which this feature does not provide.

Because a card loses width as well as height, **card coverage falls with the square of the Height percentage** while blade coverage falls in proportion to it: at Height 50% and Density 50% a card region keeps roughly an eighth of the coverage outside it, where a blade region at the same two numbers keeps a quarter. Card regions thin much faster than blade regions at identical percentages. If a card region is reading bare, raise Height before Density.

## Storage and cost

| Terrain shape | Control field | Cost when active |
|---|---|---|
| Single planar | Its native sample grid | 2 bytes per sample, CPU and GPU. A 1025 × 1025 field is 2,101,250 bytes (2.004 MiB) per copy. |
| Tiled, within the unified-texture limit | One global field on the unified lattice, independent of streaming residency | The maximum 8193 × 8193 field is about 128 MiB per copy. |
| Tiled, atlas-backed | Native control samples for resident tiles in an RG8 atlas with the existing slot and apron geometry, plus an analytic 128 × 128 global coarse field | Atlas budget accounting rises from 12 to 14 bytes per texel. The coarse texture is 32 KiB. |

Nothing is allocated while every target is `1` or no grass effect exists. The coarse field is evaluated on world coordinates, so a region on a tile that has never streamed still suppresses grass there. A tile arriving after the modifier pass uploads that coarse field first and is replaced by native samples on the next pass. Native control lattices do not follow height LOD, so adjacent resident tiles share their boundary samples exactly.

Entering or leaving the active atlas budget resizes the slot window and therefore reuploads existing terrain maps: a structural budget transition, not a heightfield edit. Content changes within the same budget upload control maps only. No per-blade instance bytes and no per-blade ECS query are added. The compat profile gains three named texture bindings, for twelve placement maps in total.

## Limits

- Planar terrain only, following the grass renderer itself. This does not enable spherical grass. The inspector says so on a sphere volume.
- Targets are bounded by `1`: a region can only reduce, never exceed the terrain's authored profile or its candidate budget.
- Coarse controls outside tile residency are an approximation of the native samples, not a promise of native precision.
- The existing single-grass-atlas-terrain limitation is unchanged.
- Height and density only. There is no per-region grass profile, no alternative blade mesh or card, and no general object scatter: a region scales the terrain's grass, it cannot swap the artwork.
- The pre-existing culling bound uses the global blade height without placement's random, clump, and card multipliers, so it can already underestimate blade height. Regional scales are bounded by one and cannot widen that gap.

:::note[Extending the control field]

Both channels are continuous and are sampled through a linear clamp sampler (`kTerrainGrassFieldChannels`, `Engine/Modules/TerrainECS/Include/TerrainECS/TerrainGrassField.h`). A discrete channel (a grass profile index, say) cannot simply join them: filtering would blend two indices into a third that names nothing. It needs a point-sampled fetch of its own, which is a change to how the field is read, not to how many fields exist.

:::
