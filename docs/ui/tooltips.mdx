---
title: Tooltips
description: Hover tooltips for game and editor UI, with text or a layout template, placement on a side of the element or of an anchor panel, and a bubble and arrow styled from CSS.
---

A tooltip is a bubble that shows when the pointer rests on an element. A small arrow on the edge of the bubble points at the element. The game UI and the editor use the same tooltip, so one stylesheet rule gives every tooltip the same look.

A tooltip holds a line of text, or a layout that you fill from code. Use a layout for rich tooltips, such as a build button with a title, a description, a cost row with icons, and a hotkey.

## Show a text tooltip

Give the element its text. The tooltip shows when the pointer rests on the element or on any element inside it.

:::::tabs
::::tab[C++]

```cpp title="UI/UIElement.h"
void SetTooltip(std::string text);
```

```cpp title="A tooltip on a button"
attackButton->SetTooltip("Attack (A)");
```

::::
::::tab[C#]

:::caution[No C# call]
C# has no call that sets a tooltip ([OpenEngine issue #82](https://github.com/Game-Crafters-Guild/OpenEngine/issues/82)). The native calls are `UIElement::SetTooltip`, `UIElement::SetTooltipPlacement`, `UIElement::SetTooltipAnchor` and `UIElement::SetTooltipTemplate`.
:::

::::
:::::

By default, the tooltip shows after the pointer rests for 0.5 seconds. When a tooltip closes and the pointer reaches another element with a tooltip within 0.5 seconds, the next tooltip shows at once. In the editor, Settings > Tooltips sets both times. A game sets them with `UIManager::SetTooltipConfigProvider`: the function it gives returns a `UIManager::TooltipConfig` with `HoverDelaySeconds` and `HoverResetDelaySeconds`. An empty text removes the tooltip.

A label that `text-overflow: ellipsis` shortens shows its full text as its tooltip, when neither the label nor an element that contains it has a tooltip of its own. An open popup, such as the list of a dropdown, does not show the tooltip of the control that opened it.

## Choose the side

The tooltip opens on the side of the element that you set. When the bubble does not fit between the element and the window edge on that side, it opens on the opposite side. The bubble always stays at least 6 px inside the window.

```cpp title="UI/UIElement.h"
enum class TooltipPlacement : uint8_t { Auto = 0, Right, Left, Above, Below };
void SetTooltipPlacement(TooltipPlacement placement);
```

| Placement | Where the bubble opens |
|---|---|
| `Auto` | Below the element. Above it when the bubble does not fit below, and it fits above or there is more room above. This is the default. |
| `Right`, `Left` | Beside the element, centered on it. |
| `Above`, `Below` | Above or below the element, centered on it. |

The arrow points at the center of the element. When the window edge pushes the bubble to one side, the arrow moves along the edge of the bubble to keep pointing at the element. It stops before the rounded corners of the bubble.

## Anchor the tooltip to a panel

A tooltip above a tile in a grid covers the tiles above it, and a tooltip of a tile at the edge of a panel can run over the next panel. Give the tile an anchor: the tooltip then sits against the anchor instead of the tile.

```cpp title="UI/UIElement.h"
void SetTooltipAnchor(UIElement* anchor);
```

The bubble opens on the side of the anchor that the placement names. It does not cover the anchor while that side has room. Along the anchor's edge, the bubble is centered on the tile. It stays inside the anchor when it fits there, and always inside the window. A bubble that is wider than the anchor is centered on the anchor. Use an anchor that holds the tile, such as the tile's panel. Null removes the anchor. When the anchor is destroyed or hidden, the tooltip sits against the tile again.

The arrow sits on the edge of the bubble that faces the anchor. It points at the center of the hovered tile: at the x of that center for a bubble above or below the anchor, and at the y of that center for a bubble on the right or left. It stops before the rounded corners of the bubble. When the pointer moves to another tile, the arrow moves to that tile. In a grid, a bubble above or below shows the column of the tile, not its row. A bubble on the right or left shows the row, not the column. Because the arrow shows only one of the two, give each tile a visible hover style so the tile is clear too.

For a bubble that stays in one place for every tile, make the tooltip as wide as the anchor. This also applies to a scaled layout: the bubble starts on the whole pixel nearest to the start of the anchor.

```cpp
// Every tile of the command card opens its tooltip above the card.
commandTile->SetTooltipPlacement(GameEngine::UIElement::TooltipPlacement::Above);
commandTile->SetTooltipAnchor(commandCard);
```

## Show a layout as the tooltip

A tooltip template is a layout file that the tooltip shows in place of a text. Give the element the asset path of the layout and a binder. Each time the tooltip shows for the element, the engine puts a new copy of the layout in the bubble and calls the binder with it. The binder fills the labels and shows or hides the parts. Then the engine sizes the bubble to its content and places it. No frame shows an empty bubble or a bubble at the wrong size.

```cpp title="UI/UIElement.h"
using TooltipBinder = std::function<void(UIElement& tooltip)>;
void SetTooltipTemplate(std::string layoutPath, TooltipBinder binder);
```

```xml title="UI/CommandTooltip.uxml"
<uielement class="command-tooltip">
  <label id="tip-title" class="tip-title"/>
  <label id="tip-description" class="tip-description"/>
  <uielement class="tip-cost-row">
    <uielement class="icon-gold"/><label id="tip-gold"/>
    <uielement class="icon-lumber"/><label id="tip-lumber"/>
  </uielement>
  <label id="tip-hotkey" class="tip-hotkey"/>
</uielement>
```

```cpp title="A build button with a template tooltip"
void SetLabel(GameEngine::UIElement& tooltip, GameEngine::StringId id, const char* text)
{
    if (auto* label = dynamic_cast<GameEngine::Label*>(tooltip.FindById(id)))
        label->SetText(text);
}

void BindBarracks(GameEngine::UIElement& tooltip)
{
    using namespace GameEngine;
    SetLabel(tooltip, "tip-title"_sid, "Barracks");
    SetLabel(tooltip, "tip-description"_sid, "Trains footmen and archers.");
    SetLabel(tooltip, "tip-gold"_sid, "150");
    SetLabel(tooltip, "tip-lumber"_sid, "50");
    SetLabel(tooltip, "tip-hotkey"_sid, "B");
}

barracksButton->SetTooltipTemplate("UI/CommandTooltip.uxml", BindBarracks);
barracksButton->SetTooltipPlacement(GameEngine::UIElement::TooltipPlacement::Above);
```

- The path is relative to the project's assets, as for any layout.
- The binder runs once each time the tooltip shows. To show new values, such as a cost the player cannot pay, read them in the binder.
- The binder receives the bubble. A class that the binder adds to the bubble leaves with the template. A style that the binder sets on the bubble stays for the tooltips that follow, so set styles on the elements of the layout, or give the bubble a class.
- The template wins over the text when an element has both. An empty path removes the template.
- When the layout does not load, the tooltip does not show, and the log names the path once.
- The bubble is at most 512 px wide.

## Style the bubble and its arrow

The tooltip is made of these elements. Style them with their classes.

| Class | Element |
|---|---|
| `.tooltip-wrapper` | The outer element that the engine places. Do not give it a size or a position. |
| `.tooltip` | The bubble: its background, border, corner radius, padding and shadow. |
| `.tooltip-text` | The label of a text tooltip. |

While the bubble shows a template, it holds the children of the layout's root element, and it also has the classes of that root element. Use them to style the bubble of one template apart from the others, for example `.command-tooltip`.

The arrow is not an element. It takes its look from the bubble:

- The fill of the arrow is the `background-color` of `.tooltip`.
- The outline of the arrow is the border color of `.tooltip`, at the border width of the edge that the arrow sits on.
- The arrow covers the border of the bubble where the two meet, so the bubble and the arrow show one outline.
- The fill of the arrow reaches 8 px past the edge of the bubble and is 9 px wide where it meets the bubble. The border goes around the fill, so a wider border makes the arrow larger and keeps the fill the same size. The tip is 8 px from the element, or from its anchor.

```css title="A framed card with a gold border, and its arrow"
.tooltip {
    background-color: #241A12;
    border-width: 2px;
    border-color: #C9A043;
    border-radius: 6px;
    padding: 8px 10px;
}

.tooltip-text {
    color: #F2E6CC;
    font-size: 14px;
}
```

Give the bubble an opaque background. The outline of the arrow is a triangle under its fill, so a translucent background lets the border color show through the arrow: at a background alpha of 0.85, the arrow shows 15 percent of the border color and the bubble shows none. With a transparent background, the arrow is a solid triangle in the border color. A `box-shadow` on the bubble does not go around the arrow.

The rules that style a tooltip come from the global stylesheets and from the stylesheets of the element's document. A game document styles its tooltips and their templates in its own stylesheet, the one its `UIDocument` names.

Without a rule, the bubble is dark gray with a gray border and light text. These colors are variables that a theme can set: `--ui_color_tooltip`, `--ui_color_tooltip_border` and `--ui_color_tooltip_text`.

## Point a popup at its control

A popup that opens from a control, such as a settings panel under a toolbar button, uses `PopoverArrow` for its arrow. Make the arrow a child of the popup, and put its box just outside the edge of the popup that faces the control. The arrow takes the fill and the border of the popup, in the same way as the tooltip arrow.

```xml title="A panel that opens below its button"
<uielement class="settings-panel">
  <PopoverArrow class="settings-arrow" side="up"/>
  <label class="settings-title" text="Camera"/>
</uielement>
```

```css title="The arrow's box, against the top edge of the panel"
.settings-arrow {
    position: absolute;
    top: -10px;
    left: 40px;
    width: 20px;
    height: 10px;
}
```

| Attribute | Values |
|---|---|
| `side` | The direction that the tip points: `up` (the default), `down`, `left` or `right`. |

The arrow runs from the outside of the popup's border to the far edge of its box, and its tip is at the middle of that edge. As for any absolute element, `top` and `left` measure from the inside of the popup's border: with `top: -10px` and a 1 px border, the tip is 9 px above the popup, so the arrow is the height of its box minus the border width. Move the box along the edge, with `left` or `top`, to point the arrow at the control.
